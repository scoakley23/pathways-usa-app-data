# AI Policy for Pathways USA

*Updated November 20, 2026 to match the app (Amio v2). This is the version shown in the app under Settings → AI policy.*

## Purpose of This Policy
This policy establishes guidelines for the responsible use of artificial intelligence at Pathways USA. An AI policy is essential to ensure that technology serves learners ethically, protects their privacy, and aligns with our mission to support adult learners preparing for the U.S. Citizenship Test.

## AI Use
AI tools are used to provide personalized study guidance and track learner progress through an application built on base44, which utilizes third-party AI platforms. These tools are designed to enhance the learning experience by adapting to individual needs and monitoring advancement toward citizenship test readiness. Annual assessments will be conducted to evaluate the impact of AI tools on learner outcomes and ensure they effectively support our educational mission.

Pathways USA is an education app, not a legal service. The AI tutor never tells a learner how they should answer a question about their own history on Form N-400, or whether they are eligible, and it points legal questions to the app's "Talk to a lawyer first" page.

## Where the AI Runs, and Daily Limits
On the new system (Supabase), every live AI request goes through Pathways USA's own server function, never straight from the learner's phone to an AI service. The requests are: Ask Amio questions without a pre-written answer, checking a spoken civics answer the phone couldn't match, scoring a practice interview answer, checking that the learner understood an interview question, and "Explain it simply" when a civics question has no reviewed explanation. The phone sends only the question and the learner's words (with emails, phone numbers, A-numbers and Social Security numbers removed); the server checks that the question is one of the app's own, writes the AI's instructions itself, and sends the request to Anthropic's Claude. Questions about current officeholders and state capitals never go to the AI. The server does not log or save the question, the answer or what the learner said.

To keep the free app affordable, each learner has a daily limit: 30 Ask Amio questions, 200 civics answer checks, 100 interview checks, and 50 other requests (UTC days). The server keeps only a count per day and type (no text), only for the latest day the learner used it, and deletes it with the account. The same count covers "Talk to a person" messages (3 a day). When a learner reaches a limit, the app says so kindly and the rest of the app keeps working (civics answers can still be checked by the learner). On the current system (base44), the same instructions go through the AI service built into base44, without these limits.

## Interactions with Learners
AI tools interact directly with learners through the application. Each learner has an AI tutor named Amio (the learner can choose its tone and how much English it uses; Amio is always a drawn character labeled "AI tutor", never a photo of a person). The tutor explains lessons, gives feedback on practice answers, builds a daily study plan, and answers study questions. Learners may contact a person at any time (see "Talk to a Person"). Feedback is gathered primarily through monitoring usage patterns and learner engagement data, supplemented by direct communication channels for questions or concerns.

## Data Collection Practices
Personal information including names, email addresses, and study progress data is collected to personalize the learning experience and track advancement. What the app saves is listed in plain words in the app (Settings → What we save) and in this policy. Learners retain full ownership of their data and may request deletion at any time, and they will be informed about their rights regarding their information.

Pathways USA never asks for or stores a learner's immigration status, A-number, case details, filing date, USCIS field office, home address, or answers to Form N-400. The one exception is "When's my interview?": if a learner chooses, their filing month and field office are kept only on their own device to show an estimate, and are never sent anywhere.

## Speech Input and Voice Data
Some practice activities let learners answer out loud (civics questions, reading sentences, the mock interview, tutor lessons, the quick check, and the N-400 lessons). Speech is converted to text by the learner's own web browser using its built-in speech recognition (for example, Google's speech service in Chrome or Apple's in Safari). Pathways USA never receives, records, or stores audio. The browser's speech provider processes the audio under its own terms and privacy policy, which Pathways USA does not control.

The resulting transcript is used only to score the answer. In reading practice the score and the tip after each try are worked out on the learner's device; nothing from reading practice is sent to an AI service. It is held temporarily in the page's memory and discarded when the learner moves on; it is never saved.

What is saved is a progress record for each practice item: best and latest score (0–100), whether the answer was correct, number of attempts, when it was last practiced, and spaced-repetition values used to decide when to review it again. This record is linked to the learner's email address, never to their voice. It powers the learner's progress charts and daily plan.

## Civics Practice Grading
In the civics quiz, civics lessons and tutor lessons, an answer (typed or spoken) that clearly matches one of the official accepted answers is checked on the learner's device and is not sent anywhere. The device check allows for how speech recognition writes words (for example "twenty-seven" or "27") and for other spellings of official answers that a person has reviewed; it never adds new answers. Other answers are graded by the AI service built into the base44 platform: the app sends the question, the learner's answer as text (with any email address, phone number, A-number or Social Security number removed), and the expected answer. The AI judges whether the meaning is correct. No name, email address, or other account information is sent. The answer itself is not saved; only the progress record described above is. In the civics lessons ("Go over them"), typed answers are checked only on the learner's device and spoken answers are graded as described here; "Explain it simply" shows the human-reviewed explanation, never one written by the AI while the learner studies, and lesson content and flashcards are translated as before but the official questions and answers are not sent for translation.

Official civics questions and accepted answers come only from the official USCIS lists in the app. The AI may explain them but never changes or adds official answers.

Some civics answers change over time (the current President, Vice President, Speaker of the House and Chief Justice) or depend on where the learner lives (their U.S. senators, U.S. representative, governor, and state capital). These answers are never sent to the AI service or checked on the web. The app grades them on the learner's device against its own list of current officeholders and capitals, which the Pathways team checks against official government websites every month; the app shows the date it was last checked. Answers that depend on where the learner lives use the state and congressional district the learner saved with "Who Represents Me?"; without one, the app asks them to use it first and doesn't count that answer as right or wrong. The AI never supplies an officeholder's name. Because answers are sent to an outside AI service, learners should answer only the civics question and not include personal information; the app shows this reminder under the answer box.

## Lesson Explanations
Lessons explain each civics question in the learner's language before practice. These explanations are written in advance, not generated while the learner studies. Pathways USA uses AI to draft them; the AI writes only the explanation and key words, never the official question or answers, which are copied from the official list. A person reviews every explanation before learners can see it, and an automated check blocks any explanation whose answers differ from the official list. Until an explanation has been reviewed, learners see "Explanation coming soon."

When a lesson is done, the learner can write their own notes for it ("My notes"). Notes are saved with their account so only they can read them, are never sent to the AI or shown to a partner program, and are erased by "Delete my data". Some course lessons can also show a short video in English from YouTube. A person reviews each video before learners can see it. Nothing is sent to YouTube until the learner taps "Play video"; then the video plays from YouTube's privacy-enhanced address (youtube-nocookie.com), and YouTube can see that the video was watched from the learner's browser, as with any web video. The app sends no account or learner information with it.

## Quick Check, Study Plan, and Target Date
**Quick check:** a short check of civics, reading, writing, and speaking. Answers are scored as described above and not saved. Only one level per skill ("Strong", "Getting there", or "Start here") and the date are saved to the learner's account, to build the study plan.

**Tutor settings:** the tutor's tone and English mix are saved to the learner's account. The tutor is always Amio, a drawn character, so there is no name or picture to choose (an old saved name is ignored). Learners could once use their own photo as the tutor picture, kept only in that device's browser storage; the app now deletes any such photo from the device when it loads ("Delete my data" also clears it). An old picture choice may remain in the account's tutor settings but is no longer shown or used.

**Course progress, stars and badges:** on the new system, the app saves each course lesson's steps done and a lesson score (0 to 3 stars), the checkpoints passed (how many right, never what was said), and when each test question comes back for review. Learners no longer see stars or badges on their screens, but stars, badges and points are still calculated and saved, so partner programs (as counts) and the pilot (as counts by a random id) keep working. "Delete my data" removes them.

**Target date and study plan:** the learner's interview or target date is saved **only on their own device** (browser storage), never to Pathways USA's database or the AI. The daily plan is calculated on the device from the learner's saved levels and progress. Daily checkmarks and visit dates are also kept only on the device. If the learner uses a new device, the app asks for the date again.

**Getting started:** before the first quick check, the app shows four short screens.
1. **Your language:** which language the learner speaks best. Saved on the device (and, once a day, with the activity date for pilot counts).
2. **How Amio works:** Amio explains in the learner's language; the learner answers, reads and writes in English, like at the interview; the learner's voice is never saved (only the words, to check the answer). Nothing is asked or saved on this screen.
3. **Your plan:** in broad terms, where the learner is in the citizenship process (getting ready to apply, sent the N-400, interview scheduled, or just learning). If they have sent it, the app asks only whether Form N-400 was sent before or after October 20, 2025, to choose the 2008 or 2025 civics test. These answers are kept **only on the learner's device** and are not sent to the AI. Then "About how long each day?": about 5, 10 or 15 minutes a day, which means 1, 2 or 3 lessons a day. Only that number of lessons is saved (to the account on the new system, on the device on the current one); minutes appear nowhere else in the app, and there are no timers.
4. **Partner program** (only when programs exist): see "Partner Programs".

The tutor is always Amio, so there is no name to choose. Getting started never asks for a filing date, case number, or field office.

**When's my interview?:** if the learner chooses, they can pick the month they filed Form N-400 and their USCIS field office to see an estimate of when cases at that office are usually finished ("Most N-400s at this office are finished between … and …. Your interview usually comes before that."). Both answers are kept **only on the learner's device** (browser storage): never saved to the account, never put in a web address, never sent to the AI, to Pathways USA or to USCIS. The time ranges come from a file a person checks by hand against the official USCIS processing times page, about once a month; the app never asks USCIS or any other site. If the times have not been checked for more than 60 days, the app stops showing them until they are checked again. Every estimate says "Estimate only. Not legal advice." The learner can add the earlier month to their plan, also on the device only. "Delete my data" clears both answers from the device.

**Reading aloud:** in onboarding, the quick check, and lessons, the tutor's messages are read aloud using the device's built-in voices. Nothing is recorded and no audio is sent anywhere. Learners can turn this off; the setting is saved on the device.

**Check-ins:** the app may suggest a shorter plan after missed days, more practice for a skill that has been hard, a short re-check every two weeks, or more English when scores are high. These are shown in the app only (no email or text messages) and are based on progress the app already saves.

## AI Mock Interviews
The mock interview feature lets learners practice with an AI-simulated USCIS officer. Learners answer out loud. As with other speech practice, the learner's browser converts speech to text using its built-in speech recognition service (see Speech Input and Voice Data above). Pathways USA never receives or stores audio.

In the Personal Information and Morals & Ethics sections, learners **do not answer** the questions. They say in their own words, in English, what the officer is asking, and the AI checks only whether they understood the question. It is told never to comment on facts about the learner's life, never to say how they should answer, and never to say anything about eligibility; if a learner answers instead, the app reminds them to explain the question. Emails, phone numbers, A-numbers and Social Security numbers are removed first. N-400 lessons uses the same understanding check, with the N-400 Guide's plain meaning of the question.

To score each answer, the app sends the text transcript, together with the interview question and the expected answer (in the understanding sections: the question and, in the N-400 lessons, its plain meaning), to the AI service built into the base44 platform. The AI scores the answer for content, grammar, and fluency. The specific AI provider behind this service is not yet fully known (see Third-Party AI Tools), and that provider's own data practices apply to the text it receives.

**What is saved:** for each completed interview, the overall score, whether it passed, and each question's section and scores, linked to the learner's email address, so the learner can see their interview history. **Transcripts, the AI's written feedback, and suggested answers are not saved**; they exist only in the page's memory for the end-of-interview report.

**Please practice with sample answers:** emails, phone numbers, A-numbers and Social Security numbers are removed from answers before they are sent, but because answers go to an outside AI service, learners should not give their real A-number, home address, case details, or other personal information during a mock interview.

## Interview Day (Full Rehearsal)
Interview Day (`/interview-day`) runs a practice version of the whole interview: the promise to tell the truth, small talk and an N-400 review where the learner says what each question is asking (never answers it), then reading, writing, and civics. It uses the same AI checks as the mock interview, reading practice, and civics practice, and sends the same things: the question, the expected answer, and what the learner said or typed (no name, email, or account information). **Nothing from a rehearsal is saved**: no scores, no progress, no records, and nothing in the browser's storage. Leaving or refreshing the page erases it. The same advice applies: don't share real personal information.

## Ask Amio (AI Tutor Questions)
"Ask Amio" answers learners' study questions in their chosen language; each answer can be switched to its English version with one tap. Answers are generated by Anthropic's Claude through Pathways USA's server on the new system, or by the AI service built into the base44 platform on the current one, with no web search (see "Where the AI Runs, and Daily Limits"). To name a current officeholder or a state capital, Amio may use only the app's own checked list: each question sends that list (public officials' names) along with it, plus the learner's saved state and congressional district from "Who Represents Me?" if they have one. Amio never asks for an address or ZIP code. On the current system the specific AI provider behind base44's service is not yet fully known; Pathways USA is reviewing base44's documentation and terms to identify it and its data practices. The questions Ask Amio suggests (for example "What are the three branches of government?", "How long is the interview?", and questions about the learner's recent lessons) have answers written and checked by people ahead of time. When the learner taps one and that answer is available in their language, it is shown and nothing is sent to the AI service; otherwise the question is answered by the AI as described above.

**What is sent:** only the learner's question and the tutor's instructions (tone, English mix, and language), plus the app's official lists: current officeholders and the official 2025 civics questions and accepted answers, so the tutor answers from them instead of from memory. Email addresses, phone numbers, A-numbers and Social Security numbers in the question are removed first; the saved history keeps the version with them removed. No account information is sent.

**Questions about the learner's own case:** questions about the learner's own immigration case, history, or eligibility are declined with a kind message and a link to "Talk to a lawyer first". Many are recognized on the device and never sent to the AI. **Declined questions are not saved.**

**What is saved:** other questions and answers are stored in the learner's study history, along with the learner's email address, chosen language, and the time of the exchange, so learners can see past conversations. Only the learner can see this history. **It is not emailed or shown to anyone else.**

**Please don't share personal details:** Ask Amio is for studying. Learners should not enter their immigration status, A-number, case details, home address, or other personal information; the app shows this reminder at the top of the chat, with the full note one tap away.

**Saying a question:** learners can tap the microphone to say their question instead of typing it. The learner's browser turns the speech into text in the chat box using its built-in speech recognition (see Speech Input and Voice Data above); Pathways USA never receives or stores audio. The learner reads the text and sends it like a typed question, and it is handled and saved exactly as described above.

## Talk to a Person
Learners can reach a person from the Ask Amio screen with a short form. The message and the learner's account email address (so we can reply) are sent by email to pathwaysusacitizenship@gmail.com. The message is not stored in the app; it is kept in that mailbox. We reply within 3–5 business days. The form reminds learners not to share immigration status, A-numbers, case details, or addresses, and that Pathways USA cannot give legal advice. Before the message is sent, the app removes email addresses, phone numbers, A-numbers and Social Security numbers from it. On the new system (Supabase), the message goes through Pathways USA's own server function and the email service Resend (which also sends the sign-in codes); the server adds the learner's account email as the reply address, and each learner can send up to 3 messages a day (only a count is kept, as for the AI limits).

## N-400 Guide and Lessons
The N-400 Guide explains each question on Form N-400 in plain language. N-400 lessons (one per section of the guide) teach learners to understand each question the way the officer may ask it: what it means, which question the officer's words match, the words that change the meaning, saying in English what the question asks (the AI checks only their understanding, never an answer), and how to ask for help. There are no sample answers. The app never collects, fills in, or stores a learner's answers to Form N-400. Only the lesson's steps and scores and which sections the learner has completed are saved. Each lesson ends with a reminder to always tell the truth and to talk to a lawyer before filing if "yes" might be true for them.

## Representative Lookup and Addresses
The "Who Represents Me?" civics feature helps learners learn the names of their U.S. senators, U.S. representative and governor, and their state capital, which can be asked on the citizenship test. It starts with a zip code, which the app looks up on the learner's device in a table published by the U.S. Census Bureau (zip code areas by congressional district); the zip code is not sent anywhere. Only if that zip code spans more than one congressional district, or isn't in the Census table, does the app ask for a street address to find the exact district. The street address and zip code are then sent once to find the state and congressional district, never names: on the new system to Geocodio (a U.S. address lookup service), through Pathways' own server so no account information goes with it; on the current system to the AI service built into the base44 platform (which may search the web; see Third-Party AI Tools). That provider's own data practices apply to what it receives. The app checks the result against its own list; if the district isn't on it, nothing is saved and the learner is asked to try again. The names shown come from the app's own list of current officeholders, checked against official government websites every month.

The street address is used only for that lookup, in the moment, and is then cleared; the app says so next to the address box. Pathways USA never saves it to the user's profile or anywhere else. The only results the app keeps are the state and the congressional district, saved on the user's profile so civics practice and Amio can name the right officials. Names are never saved. (Profiles saved before this change had officials' names; the app removes them and keeps only the state and district.) They are erased with the rest of the profile when the user chooses "Delete my data."

Pathways USA does not keep home addresses because, for people going through the immigration process, a stored address linked to their account could put them or their households at risk.

## Resource Hub (Help Near You)
The Resource Hub shows nearby free legal help, English and citizenship classes, USCIS offices, and similar places on a map and in a list. The learner can share their location through the browser (the browser asks permission first) or type a ZIP code. The location is used only on the learner's device, in the moment, to sort places by distance: it is not saved to the account, to the device, or anywhere else, and it is not sent to the AI service or to base44. ZIP codes are turned into map coordinates using a table built into the app, so a typed ZIP code is not sent anywhere either.

The map pictures come from OpenFreeMap (a free public map service using OpenStreetMap data), or, on devices that can't draw that map, from the U.S. Geological Survey's National Map; like any web map, the service receives requests for the area being shown. "Search this area" uses the center of the map on the device only, like a typed ZIP code. "Directions" opens Google Maps with only the place's public address; the maps app asks for the starting point itself. When a learner opens a place's summary on the map, that place's logo (if it has one) loads from the National Literacy Directory's website or the place's own website; no referrer or learner information is sent with it.

Signed-in learners can tap "Save" on a place to keep it in "Your saved places" and find it again later, on any device. Only the place's id is saved, linked to the learner's email address; their location, ZIP code and searches are still never saved. Only the learner can see their saved places (not partners or the Pathways team), they can remove one at any time, and "Delete my data" removes them all. Saved places are not sent to the AI service.

The list of places is written and checked by the Pathways team from public sources (such as the USCIS office list, the U.S. Department of Justice list of recognized organizations, and the National Literacy Directory's public class listings). The AI does not find, write, or recommend places. Pathways USA does not run or endorse these places, and the page reminds learners that only lawyers and DOJ-accredited representatives can give immigration legal advice.

## Partner Programs
Some learners study with a class or program that works with Pathways USA (a "partner program"). In Getting started, and later on their Profile, a learner may say which partner program they study with, or that they study on their own. This is optional. A partner's link can pick the program in advance; the program's code waits in the browser's storage until the learner answers.

**What is saved:** which program the learner picked, whether the program has confirmed them, an optional name the learner gives so the program knows it's them (a name only; anything that looks like an email address or a number is dropped), and, only where the program allows it, whether the learner chose to share their own progress. This record is linked to the learner's email address.

**What the program sees:** the program's staff see a list of the learners who picked their program, showing the name the learner gave, or their email address if they gave none. Staff confirm each learner or mark them "not ours". They also see totals over their confirmed learners, as exact numbers: how many there are, how many opened the app in the last 7 and 30 days (counted, never shown per learner), how many finished the quick check and at which levels, how many lessons and N-400 Guide sections they finished, their lesson scores (a count of stars), and how many checkpoints they passed (each checkpoint counted once). Where a program has it turned on, a learner may choose to share their own progress with it; the program then sees that learner's quick check levels and how many lessons, stars, checkpoints and N-400 sections they have, nothing more, and the learner can turn it off in Settings (My tutor). Programs never see learners' answers, practice or mock interview results, Ask Amio conversations, notes, pilot check-in answers, or anything about their case. Programs only ever see learners who picked them.

**Changing or leaving:** learners can change or leave their program in Settings (My tutor) at any time, and "Delete all my data" removes the record. None of this is sent to the AI service.

## Pilot Check-ins and Outcome Questions
Pathways USA asks learners a few optional questions to learn whether the app helps. At sign-up, a learner may share their state, where they are in the citizenship process (not filed yet, filed, or interview scheduled), and how ready they feel. At 3, 6 and 12 months, the app may ask whether they have filed, had their interview, passed the tests, taken the oath and registered to vote. Every question is optional and has an "I'd rather not say" choice. The app never asks for dates, case numbers, A-numbers, addresses or immigration status, and has no free-text boxes for these questions.

**Who can see the answers:** only Pathways USA administrators. Partner staff cannot see any learner's answers.

**Reminder emails:** only if the learner turns them on. The emails do not mention citizenship, immigration or the interview. Learners can turn them off in Settings (Help us improve Amio).

**Activity dates:** the app records which days a learner opened it (the date only, not what they did) to measure how often people practice. It also keeps the code of the language the learner uses the app in, to see which language groups the app reaches.

**Exports and reports:** data used for analysis uses a random ID, never names or email addresses. Administrators see these answers only in a pilot dashboard that lists learners by that random ID and shows totals; the downloadable file has one row per learner by random ID, with the sign-up week instead of the date, and stays with Pathways USA. Pathways USA may share aggregate results (for example, "18 of 30 learners finished a mock interview") with partners, funders and government agencies. It does not sell or license individual learner data.

**Deletion:** "Delete all my data" erases check-in answers, activity dates and these profile fields.

**How the check-ins work:** a 3-, 6- or 12-month check-in shows as a card on the learner's Today page for 30 days; a missed one is not asked later. The learner can put it off ("Not now") or stop all check-ins ("Don't ask me again", which also stops reminder emails) and turn them back on in Profile. Milestones already answered "Yes" are not asked again. A learner whose interview is scheduled is also asked once, before a practice mock interview, how ready they feel (not again for 30 days).

**Reminder emails, in detail:** an administrator sends due reminders from the Admin page. Each learner gets at most one email per check-in, and only if they turned reminders on. Pathways keeps an administrator-only record of which reminder was sent to which email address and when, so no one gets two; learners and partner staff cannot see it. While reminders are on, the account also keeps the learner's app language so the email can be in that language; on the current system the fixed email text is translated by the AI service (no learner information is sent), and on the new system it comes from the app's translations prepared ahead of time (English otherwise). The emails are sent through base44's email service on the current system, and through Resend on the new one.

None of the learners' answers are sent to the AI service.

## Translations of App Text
Fixed app text and study content (for example, menu labels, the tutor's standard messages, civics questions, lessons and the N-400 guide) are translated ahead of time, before release, and shipped with the app, so nothing is sent to an AI service to translate them while the learner studies. Text not yet in that set is shown in English on the new system; on the current system it may still be translated by the AI service and saved on the learner's device so it is translated only once. English and Spanish versions of key messages are written by people. No learner text is included in these translations.

## Ethical Risks and Concerns
Potential risks include bias in study recommendations that could disadvantage certain learner groups and privacy concerns related to the handling of personal information. Measures will be implemented to detect and prevent bias, with annual audits conducted to assess fairness in AI outputs.

## Accountability for AI Decisions
The founder of Pathways USA will oversee all AI-related decisions and ensure adherence to this policy. This includes monitoring AI tool performance, reviewing AI-drafted lesson explanations before they are published, addressing ethical concerns, and maintaining alignment with the organization's mission to support adult learners.

## Third-Party AI Tools
The application utilizes base44, which incorporates third-party AI platforms for development and functionality. While the specific AI tools embedded within base44 are not fully known, efforts will be made to review the terms of service and data practices of third-party providers (including whether they retain or train on data they receive) to ensure they align with our ethical standards and learner protection commitments. On the new system the AI provider is Anthropic (Claude), reached only through Pathways USA's server function with Pathways USA's own API key; Pathways USA reviews Anthropic's commercial terms and data-retention practices. Speech recognition is provided by the learner's own browser, as described above.

## Staff Training and Guidance
As a sole-staff organization, the founder will manage all AI tools and maintain knowledge of AI ethics, limitations, and best practices. Written guidelines will be developed as the organization grows to ensure consistent and responsible AI use.

## Bias Audits and Fairness
Annual bias audits will be conducted to identify and address any disparities in how AI tools serve different learner populations. These audits will examine study recommendations, progress tracking, and engagement patterns to ensure equitable access and outcomes for all learners.

## Data Ownership and Learner Rights
Learners retain full ownership of their data and will be clearly informed about their rights, including the ability to request data deletion at any time. Deletion applies to data held by Pathways USA. Messages a learner sent through the "Talk to a person" form are kept in the Pathways USA mailbox and can be deleted on request.

**How deletion works:** the app's "Delete my data" option erases everything tied to the person's email address: Ask Amio history, notes, practice progress, lesson progress, mock interview history, N-400 Guide progress, saved places, pilot check-in answers, activity dates, the record of reminder emails sent, partner program choice, notifications, and profile. It also resets the account's tutor settings, quick check levels, points, badges, display name, state, app language, reminder and check-in settings, and clears the app's data on that device (including the target date, study plan, and the filing month and field office from "When's my interview?"). On the new system, it also deletes the sign-in account itself. The sign-in account itself is deleted on request by emailing pathwaysusacitizenship@gmail.com.

## Legal and Regulatory Compliance
Pathways USA is in the process of determining which data protection laws apply to its operations and how to ensure full compliance. This includes reviewing requirements under COPPA, GDPR, and other relevant regulations to protect learner information and maintain legal standards.

## Public Transparency and Reporting
Information about AI use is published in this AI policy, which learners can read in the app (Settings → AI policy), together with the plain list of what the app saves (Settings → What we save). Pathways USA does not have a separate privacy policy or terms of service yet; this section will name them when they exist. Annual impact assessments will inform updates to this policy and ensure ongoing transparency about how AI tools are used and their effects on learners.

## Monitoring and Continuous Improvement
AI tools will be monitored continuously through learner engagement data and feedback received via direct communication channels. Annual impact assessments will evaluate whether AI tools are meeting educational goals and serving learners effectively, with adjustments made as needed to improve outcomes and address any identified concerns.
